## The standard sample

The same queries on every case, run on the broken warehouse after the nightly build: row counts per table, the raw
column list (`information_schema`), monthly totals of `orders` with its per-method columns, `orders` by status, monthly
totals of `daily_revenue`, the `customers` totals, the seven `region_revenue` rows, and ten rows of every table picked
by `order by hash(t) limit 10`, so the rows are fixed by the data and not by anyone. The symptom text stays per case.
The prompt is about 47 KB against 29 KB before. Two scratch copies of the project ran 16 cases each, nine calls at a
time, 23:47 to 00:11; one Opus call on `rename_and_utc` gave no answer in 300 s and was re-run alone against the
frozen prompt (same sha as the other eight). Cost USD 29.72 (Sonnet 9.52, Haiku 0.79, Opus 19.41).

Scoring: the same two questions and the same rule (a defect named as the conclusion with a check attached is found; a
defect offered as one of two readings or handed back is not). Each case's nine runs were read against the ground truth
and the case's Verification paragraph from the curated scoring; seven fix shapes not covered there were checked against
the injected seeds (`amount % 100 <> 0` on two cases, `amount >= 1000000`, a Sydney offset that moves nine orders, a
suffix regexp that never fires, a `where order_date is not null` that is a no-op after the reload, a `distinct` over the
lookup that works because both rates are equal). One rule was tried and dropped: a rebuild selector that skips the model
the run edited (`dbt build -s daily_revenue+ orders+` after editing `stg_payments.sql`) is noted in the score files but
not scored, because twelve curated runs with the same slip were scored on the fix shape, and the comparison has to use
one rule. Verdicts are still one reader's; the blind sample is the check.

## What it says

1. **The curated queries were worth 6 found and 8 fixed of 288, and the aggregate hides where.** Twenty cells moved.
   The drop sits in the two families the curated queries used to feed: the mixed cases (73 to 67 found) and the
   timezone cases (24 to 22, and `rename_and_utc` alone from 8 of 9 fixed to 0).
2. **Which rows the hash picks decides some cases.** `rename_and_utc` lost because the standard sample never shows
   order 73's warehouse timestamp, so the 5h30 cannot be derived: seven runs took the equal January and February
   pairs as proof that the new column shares the clock, and the two Sonnet runs that committed to UTC guessed
   Australia/Sydney from the region names and moved nine orders a day late. `rename_and_new_status` doubled (3 to 6 of
   9) because the ten `raw_orders` rows happened to hold two `delivered` rows beside `completed` ones, and
   `join_drops_rows` went to 9 of 9 because a NULL `order_id` row landed in both the raw and the staging sample. A
   fixed sample is fair across cases and not neutral within one. For the paper: both samples reported, the standard
   numbers as the headline, the curated ones as the "someone pointed at it" ceiling.
3. **The hand-back is unchanged.** `duplicates_and_rename` is 0 of 9 on both samples. On the curated sample every run
   saw payment 114 beside payment 68 and handed it back; on the standard one the pair is not in view, no run considered
   duplicates, and all nine read the copies' only trace (`orders` 708.00 and 146.00 against `daily_revenue` 622.00 and
   139.00) backwards, as a stale dashboard to refresh.
4. **Haiku moves most.** 77 to 73 found and 72 to 66 fixed; Sonnet and Opus move by one to three. Opus's three losses
   are one `fanout_and_late` run ("the raw data is fine"), the whole of `rename_and_utc`, and a `utc_switch_blind`
   response that is forty unexecuted tool calls hunting for the seed with tools off and no diagnosis.
5. **The stale dashboard is now the commonest red herring.** With `daily_revenue` monthly totals in every prompt, most
   runs on the payment cases read its clean figures as stale history to rebuild rather than as the one healthy
   reference, and said so even when it did not change their fix.
